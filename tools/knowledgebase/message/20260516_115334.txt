RESPONSE
========================================================================
Based on your trace, disassembly, and emulator architecture, the issue is not with the BIOS ignoring the keystroke, but rather an **emulator bug** causing execution to get permanently stuck inside the INT 9 ISR, preventing it from ever reaching the BDA write instructions.

Here are the answers to your questions and the missing pieces to get this working:

### 1. The "caller at F000:E9CB" Pattern (The Root Cause)
This pattern is **not expected** and is the smoking gun. `F000:E9CB` corresponds to the bytes `2E D7`, which is `XLAT CS:`. 
If your timer or keyboard IRQs are *always* logging `F000:E9CB` as the interrupted `CS:IP`, it means the CPU is stuck in an infinite loop at that exact instruction. 

**Why is it stuck?** In custom x86 emulators, a very common bug is failing to advance the Instruction Pointer (`IP`) correctly when handling prefixed instructions. If your CPU fetch loop decodes the `2E` (CS override) prefix, fetches the `D7` (XLAT) opcode, executes it, but **fails to increment IP** past the whole 2-byte instruction sequence, the CPU will execute `XLAT CS:` forever.
Because `XLAT` doesn't change `IF`, interrupts still fire. When IRQ 0 (Timer) fires, it saves `CS:E9CB`, handles the timer, and IRETs back to the infinite loop at `E9CB`. This perfectly explains why execution never reaches the BDA write at `EA24`/`EB6A`, and why FreeDOS seemingly stays alive (timer ticks still process).

*Check your CPU core's implementation of opcode prefixes and ensure `IP` properly skips both the prefix and the opcode.*

### 2. Is there a known case where it skips BDA writes?
The BIOS will skip writing to the BDA if the buffer is full (head == next tail) or if the keystroke represents an unhandled modifier state. However, for a normal Enter keystroke, it should definitely write. 
The reason it isn't writing is due to the infinite loop mentioned above. 

Additionally, your trace shows `XLAT CS:` resulting in `AL = 0x3B`. **This is incorrect.** `0x3B` is the ASCII code for `;` (and the scancode for F1). An Enter key (`0x1C`) passed through the unshifted table at `E885` should yield `0x0D` (Carriage Return). 
You should review your `XLAT` instruction logic (`AL = Memory.Read(Segment, BX + AL)`). It is likely reading from the wrong physical address, failing to cast `AL` as an unsigned 8-bit offset, or adding `AH` by mistake. 

### 3. Edge-triggering and Raising IRQ 1
Your model of edge-asserting once per KeyDown works for getting the *first* interrupt, but it bypasses XT hardware reality. The BIOS expects ONE hardware IRQ per scancode byte. You should inject the scancode into your port 0x60 FIFO, and assert the IRQ 1 line HIGH. You leave it HIGH until the BIOS acknowledges it (see point 4). 

### 4. The "Ack Pulse" on Port 0x61 (Crucial for XT)
Treating port 0x61 as a plain RAM byte is a critical missing piece for hardware accuracy. On the PC/XT architecture:
* When the BIOS writes to port `0x61` with **bit 7 set (1)**, the keyboard controller hardware clears its internal shift register AND forces the IRQ 1 line **LOW** (de-asserting the interrupt).
* The BIOS then writes to `0x61` with **bit 7 cleared (0)** to allow the keyboard to clock in the next key.

To accurately emulate this: when your emulator detects an `OUT 0x61` where bit 7 transitions to `1`, you should pop/clear your active scancode logic and immediately de-assert your 8259A's IRQ 1 line. Without this, a real BIOS would never receive a second keystroke, as the IRQ line would stay high forever (preventing future rising edges).

### 5. BIOS-side Keyboard Init Phase
For the PC/XT, there is no bidirectional initialization phase like there is for AT/PS2 (8042) keyboards. The XT keyboard simply sends clock and data lines directly. The POST just zeros out the BDA and expects scancodes to stream in.

### Summary Checklist to Fix:
1. Fix your CPU core so that instruction prefixes (like `2E CS:`) properly advance `IP` past the underlying opcode.
2. Debug your `XLAT` instruction logic — `E885 + 0x1C` should yield `0x0D`, not `0x3B`.
3. Hook `OUT 0x61` so that setting bit 7 explicitly de-asserts IRQ 1.
