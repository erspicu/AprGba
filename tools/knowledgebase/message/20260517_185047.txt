This is a fantastic divergence to debug. The clues you provided point to an incredibly subtle and beautiful cascade of failures. 

**Both the JIT and INTERP actually share a critical bug**, but this bug triggers a **Memory Bank Switch**, which causes the two execution backends to diverge!

Here is the exact breakdown of what is happening.

### 1. The Root Bug: 8-bit Register Setters (Low Bytes)
Look closely at the `HL` and `DE` registers in your post-states. 
You noted that A and D were loaded with `$4D`, which looks suspiciously like the block-start `L` value. You are exactly right, but it's not just a stale read. 

Your `WriteReg8` logic for the **low half** of 16-bit registers (L, E, C, F) has a bitwise shift bug. It is likely doing this:
```csharp
// BUGGY Low-byte setter
HL = (ushort)((HL << 8) | new_L);
```
When it should be doing this:
```csharp
// CORRECT Low-byte setter
HL = (ushort)((HL & 0xFF00) | new_L);
```
Because of `(HL << 8)`, **the old Low byte is shifted into the High byte**, overwriting it! 

Let's trace what this did to your block:
* **Pre-state:** `HL = $014D` (H=`$01`, L=`$4D`)
* **Step 1 (`ADD A,L`):** `$4F + $4D` -> `A=$9C`.
* **Step 2 (`LD L,A`):** You write `$9C` to L. Due to the bug, `HL = (0x014D << 8) | 0x9C`. `HL` becomes **`$4D9C`**! (`H` is now overwritten with the old `L` value, `$4D`).

This buggy `HL=$4D9C` propagates through the rest of the block:
* **Step 6 (`LD E,H`):** Reads H (`$4D`). E becomes `$4D`.
* **Step 10 (`LD D,H`):** Reads H (`$4D`). D becomes `$4D`. (Notice that INTERP's DE is exactly `$4D4D`!).
* **Step 16 (`LD A,H`):** Reads H (`$4D`). A becomes `$4D`.

### 2. The Bank Switch
At **Step 15 (`LD (HL),A`)**, the CPU writes the value of A (`$4D`) to the address in `HL`. 
Because `HL` was corrupted, it doesn't write to RAM at `$019C`. Instead, it writes to **`$4D9C`**.

Address `$4D9C` falls squarely into the MBC ROM area (`$4000-$5FFF`). Writing to this address triggers a **Memory Bank Controller (MBC)** register write (likely switching the active ROM bank!).

### 3. The Divergence
When the bank switches mid-block, the memory backing `$4000-$7FFF` instantly changes. 
* **The JIT Backend:** Already translated the whole 18-instruction block reading the *old* memory. It doesn't know the bank switched. It happily natively executes the old Steps 16, 17, and 18 (ending with `LD D,L` which gives JIT its final `DE=$9C4D`), and linearly lands at `PC=$4E09`.
* **The INTERP Backend:** Fetches dynamically. At Step 16, it fetches instructions from the **NEW ROM bank**. Whatever random bytes are in the newly swapped bank at `$4E06`, `$4E07`, and `$4E08` are executed! 
   * As a fun theory, the new ROM bank probably happened to have an `ADD A, C` instruction (which mathematically yields exactly `A=$60`, `F=$20` based on `A=$4D` + `C=$13`) followed by a jump or RST instruction that threw INTERP to `PC=$003A`.

### To Answer Your Key Questions:
1. **Why INTERP landed at $003A:** The bank switch replaced the underlying memory. INTERP dynamically fetched a control-flow instruction (like an `RST` or `JP`) from the new bank that the JIT never saw.
2. **Side effect from an instruction?** Yes! `LD (HL),A` had the side effect of triggering an MBC Bank Switch, but *only* because `HL` was horribly corrupted by Step 2.
5. **JIT's F=$80:** Came from Step 8 (`SUB A,A` -> A=0, F=$C0) and Step 9 (`ADC A,B` -> A=0+0, zero flag set -> F=$80). JIT perfectly executed the linear block.

### Concrete Next Steps:
You don't need (A), (B), (C), or (D) just yet! 
1. **Fix your 8-bit register setters:** Audit how you write to `C`, `E`, `L`, and `F` in your CPU state representation. Ensure you are masking with `& 0xFF00` instead of shifting `<< 8`. 
2. *(Future proofing)*: Mid-block bank switches are a notorious edge case in JIT Game Boy emulators. Real games will do this intentionally. If `EmitWriteMem` hits an MBC register and switches the bank, the JIT needs to detect this and early-exit the block to prevent executing stale translated code.
