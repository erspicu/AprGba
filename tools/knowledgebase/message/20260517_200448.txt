This is a classic "Sherlock Holmes" debugging scenario. The IR you dumped is perfectly correct, the `RETI` logic is sound, and your recent fixes in Q3 are entirely innocent. 

The smoking gun is exactly what you noticed: **JIT POST PC is exactly `$0048`**, which is ONE LESS than `i6_pre`'s pre-write of `$0049`. 

Here is the fresh perspective on what is actually happening.

### The Core Diagnosis (Answering Q1 & Q2)
The only mathematically possible way for the `block_exit` drain to read `PC=$0048` and `SP=$6CB0` when `i6_exec` clearly contains the stores for `$6F9A` and `$6CB2` is this: **`i6_exec` never executed.**

Furthermore, `i6_pre` didn't fully execute either. 
1. `i5` (`ADD HL, HL` at `$0047`) is a 1-byte instruction. Its pre-write in `i5_pre` wrote `0047 + 1 = $0048` to `pc_local`.
2. `i5` finishes, and control flows to `i6_pre`.
3. At the very top of `i6_pre` (before clearing `PcWritten` and before updating `pc_local` to `$0049`), there is almost certainly a **Cycle Budget Check**. 
4. The budget check **failed**. The JIT immediately aborted the block and jumped to `block_exit`.
5. `block_exit` drained the completely untouched `sp_local` (`$6CB0` from the block start) and the untouched `pc_local` (`$0048` left over from `i5_pre`).

This perfectly explains both Q1 and Q2. The instructions weren't optimized away by LLVM; they were legally bypassed by JIT control flow.

### Why did JIT run out of budget while INTERP executed RETI?
If both the JIT and INTERP were given the exact same cycle budget by the fuzzer, why did they diverge? 

Look at `i0`: **`JP NZ, $D9B6`**. The condition was false (Z=1). 
In the Game Boy (LR35902), `JP cc, a16` takes **16 cycles if taken**, but only **12 cycles if not taken**. 

If your JIT cycle-deduction logic accidentally deducts a fixed 16 cycles for `JP cc` regardless of the branch outcome (or deduces 16 in `i0_pre` without refunding 4), the JIT will believe it has consumed **4 extra cycles** compared to INTERP. 

Let's do the math:
* **INTERP Cycles (up to i6):** 12 (JP untaken) + 16 (LD) + 4 (LD) + 4 (CP) + 4 (LD) + 8 (ADD) = **48 cycles**.
* **JIT Cycles (up to i6):** 16 (JP taken bug) + 16 + 4 + 4 + 4 + 8 = **52 cycles**.

If the fuzzer fed a tight budget to execute the block (e.g., exactly 64 cycles), INTERP reaches `RETI` with 16 cycles left. Since `RETI` takes 16 cycles, INTERP happily executes it. The JIT, however, reaches `i6_pre` needing 16 cycles but only has 12 left. It fails the budget check and bails out cleanly exactly at the boundary of `$0048`.

### Addressing Q3: Recent Fixes
Your recent fixes are solid. 
* Setting `ctx.PcWriteEmittedInCurrentInstruction = true` at JIT time for `Ret` safely ensures that `BlockFunctionBuilder` generates an `i6_post` that checks `PcWritten == 1` and cleanly ends the block. 
* SyncEmitter skipping the overwrite when `PcWritten == 1` is completely correct for a block-terminating instruction.
None of these caused the bug because the execution aborted before reaching them.

### Addressing Q4: Pre-write Masking
The pre-write of `$0049` is not masking `RETI`'s pop. If `i6_pre` had actually reached the pre-write line, `PC` would have been drained as `$0049` (or `$6F9A` if `i6_exec` ran). The fact that it drained as `$0048` is the absolute proof that the top of `i6_pre` was the end of the line.

### How to Fix
1. Verify your JIT cycle deduction logic for the LR35902 `JP cc, a16` instruction. Ensure it deducts exactly 12 cycles when the condition is false. (This often trips developers up because standard Z80 `JP cc, nn` is a fixed 10 cycles, but LR35902 makes it variable).
2. If `JP cc` is already correct, look for any other cycle divergence in `ADD HL, HL` or the `LD` instructions, but `JP cc` is highly likely the culprit here.
